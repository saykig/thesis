## Questions to retain

Status: **develop** = priority for narrowing; **open** = unresolved;
**supporting** = potentially useful explanation; **parked** = preserve outside
the paper's likely scope at the time. These labels record past assessments,
not a current ranking or topic choice.

| ID | Question | Distinction or test needed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q01 | Why join a defence alliance when national interests differ? | Separate deterrence and pooled capabilities from credibility, influence and the political costs of acting alone. Compare membership with a credible outside option; do not assume joining is inevitable. | Supporting |
| Q02 | When does a common threat produce cooperation? | Measure perceived threat, geographic exposure and proposed response separately. Sharing an adversary does not establish agreement about spending, escalation or deployment. | Develop |
| Q03 | Is the problem free riding, coordination, bargaining or commitment? | A public-goods problem concerns contribution incentives; assurance concerns confidence in others; bargaining concerns division; commitment concerns future reliability. Establish the payoff logic before selecting a prisoner's dilemma. | Develop |
| Q04 | Does alliance enlargement necessarily increase friction? | Added vetoes and preference diversity may raise costs; added capabilities, expertise and established routines may offset them. Member count alone cannot identify a mechanism. Future enlargement is not assumed. | Open |
| Q05 | Can inclusion coexist with national distinctiveness? | Brewer concerns individual social identification. Test any bridge through leaders' identity, status or domestic legitimacy; distinguish these from a state's institutional autonomy. No automatic person-to-state transfer. | Supporting |
| Q06 | What does a shared institutional foundation actually mean? | Distinguish common vocabulary, technical standards, evidence provenance, decision procedures and a single objective function. The first four do not require identical preferences or a universal policy. | Develop |
| Q07 | Does better information reduce disagreement? | It may correct mistaken beliefs, reveal incompatible interests, or enable selective disclosure. Observe belief change and agreement separately. Accurate transmission is not proof of truthful reporting. | Develop |
| Q08 | Which information must be shared, with whom and when? | Map acquisition, classification, release permission, verification, timing and access revocation. Compare the information relevant to a decision with the volume transmitted. | Develop |
| Q09 | Does an integrated network improve coordination or create dependence? | Map alternate routes and provider control, not just connectivity. An efficient hub may also be an access gatekeeper or single point of failure. | Develop |
| Q10 | Can trade, finance, mobility or other benefits sustain defence cooperation? | Treat these as distinct institutional layers and possible bargaining links. Identify an actual conditional exchange; do not treat every EU benefit as a NATO instrument. | Open |
| Q11 | How do institutions preserve cooperation among unequal regional stakes? | Ask what makes participation acceptable: reciprocity, reputation, specialist contributions, compensation or delegation. Italy's Arctic involvement contradicts a simple no-interest assumption. | Develop |
| Q12 | How should Hungary and Belarus enter the comparison? | Hungary is an internal EU/NATO member case; Belarus is outside both institutions. Internal blocking and external alignment require different mechanisms and evidence. Geography is not membership. | Supporting |
| Q13 | Should Russia and China be treated as one common threat? | No starting equivalence. Distinguish direct military threat, economic exposure, technology dependence and regional activity; investigate national perceptions rather than infer them from NATO membership. | Supporting |
| Q14 | Does uncertainty about US support change European cooperation? | Possible responses include more European coordination, bilateral hedging or reduced trust. Measure dependence and credible alternatives; do not assume a single response. | Open |
| Q15 | Do material conditions determine diplomacy, or do leaders reshape them? | Trace a sequence linking infrastructure/dependence, perceived options, leader choices and subsequent investment. Reciprocal causation is plausible; a secondhand account cannot establish that Pomeroy's argument is wrong. | Open |
| Q16 | Is a chip economy replacing an oil economy? | Retain the intuition as changing strategic dependence, not an established replacement. Define a sector, bottleneck and observable policy response; energy and computing can be complementary dependencies. | Parked |
| Q17 | Can formal rules preserve room for personal diplomacy and serendipity? | Separate credible institutional commitments from discretion in proposing bargains and building trust. Test whether informal contact changes beliefs or terms within constraints. Neither algorithmic control nor pure spontaneity is assumed. | Open |
| Q18 | What can neuroeconomics contribute? | Individual reciprocity, reward, trust and risk may inform a micro-mechanism. Brain-imaging results do not establish state preferences or institutional outcomes. Requires relevant behavioural evidence; neural data are unnecessary for the current design. | Parked |
| Q19 | What can information science contribute beyond information theory? | Classification, provenance, knowledge organization, access rules and shared meanings are possible institutional mechanisms. Identify a documented release or interpretation problem before prescribing an ontology or tool. | Supporting |
| Q20 | Can findings transfer across Ukraine, NATO, EU defence and the High North? | Transfer a mechanism only when actors, actions, information rights and incentives match. Similar vocabulary or a reusable graph is insufficient. The Baltic is not interchangeable with the Arctic. | Open |
| Q21 | Does decision speed improve decision quality across repeated cycles? | Preserve the original conflict–decision–implementation–feedback question. Separate agreement time from implementation time, quality, losses and resource use. Faster agreement is not necessarily better defence. | Open |
| Q22 | How do AI decision-support systems change officers' judgment in theater-level decisions? | Test whether speed, trust in recommendations and provider control alter meaningful human deliberation or accountability. Link any coalition claim to an observed European decision episode; see the [5 November idea note](HUMAN_AI_MILITARY_DECISIONS.md). | Develop |
| Q23 | Is there a universally dominant model for cooperation? | Seek conditional, reusable concepts and explicit assumptions. Do not claim one institution, topology or algorithm dominates all objectives, threats and distributions of power. | Open |
| Q24 | Where might this paper add something to existing IR? | A possible contribution concerns when information arrangements reduce coordination costs versus redistribute bargaining leverage. Establish what existing literature already explains before claiming novelty. | Develop |

## A 3 November candidate question (historical)

> Under what conditions does shared information facilitate joint defence
> decisions among European allies, and when does control over that information
> become a source of bargaining leverage?

This was a candidate direction, not a final paper question. Sara has now set
aside a single organizing question while exploring the interests in the
[README timeline](../../README.md#interests-so-far). The candidate contained
two mechanisms; a 20-page paper would need a narrower primary outcome.

A bounded version proposed at the time was:

> How did control over a specified information channel shape the terms of a
> particular coalition-support negotiation?

An alternative focused on integration would ask whether preserving national
release authority made one specific sharing arrangement acceptable. The latter
requires negotiations and participation evidence that have not yet been secured.

## Candidate hypotheses from the 3 November framing

| Proposition | Mechanism | Observation that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Shared, credible information facilitates agreement mainly when disagreement concerns beliefs about conditions rather than desired outcomes. | Updating reduces an informational obstacle to a mutually acceptable action. | Beliefs converge without improved agreement, or agreement changes only after compensation, coercion or a policy concession. |
| H2: Dependence on a difficult-to-replace information provider gives that provider bargaining leverage when access can credibly be restricted. | Restricted access worsens the recipient's outside option. | Effective substitutes exist, the restriction is not credible, or bargaining changes precede it. Concurrent weapons or financial pressure may prevent identifying a separate information effect. |
| H3: Retaining national discretion over participation or information release can facilitate adoption of a shared arrangement, while limiting later collective use. | Lower initial sovereignty costs can coexist with later coordination costs. | Negotiating records show discretion was irrelevant to joining, or release restrictions do not affect the defined operation. Selection into flexible arrangements must be addressed. |

None of these is new merely because it is restated here. Literature review must
identify the existing scope, predictions and evidence before any novelty claim.

## Institutional checks

- NATO has [32 members](https://nato.int/en/about-us/organization/nato-member-countries)
  and uses [consensus without voting](https://nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/consensus-decision-making-at-nato).
  Its own account says regular consultation often makes negotiations rapid.
  Treat “larger means slower” as a hypothesis, not an established law.
- EU [CSDP decisions generally require unanimity](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum:european_security_defence_policy),
  with specified exceptions. This is distinct from
  [other Council voting rules](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/council-eu/how-does-the-council-vote/).
  Currency, mobility, trade and defence do not form a single uniform authority.
- [PESCO](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/pesco/) combines a common
  framework with varying project membership. This documents differentiated
  participation, not its effectiveness.
- The [2022 NATO Strategic Concept](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2022/06/29/nato-leaders-approve-new-strategic-concept)
  distinguishes Russia's direct threat from the challenges posed by China.
  An agreed text does not measure every government's threat perception.
- Hungary appears on the EU/PESCO and NATO membership lists. Belarus is outside
  both; NATO's [Belarus account](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/partnerships-and-cooperation/relations-with-belarus)
  documents suspended partnership activity and attributes complicity in Russia's
  invasion to Belarus.
- NATO's [6 June 2026 X-Arctic announcement](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2026/06/06/nato-launches-task-force-x-arctic-to-strengthen-awareness-in-the-arctic-and-the-high-north)
  identifies the Italian Navy as operating the experimental vessel.
  This is participation evidence and an institutional programme account, not
  evidence of successful coordination or a complete account of Italian motives.

## Proposed next step from the 3 November framing

The following was a possible research path, not the current work plan.

Choose one decision episode after a short evidence audit. For each candidate,
identify who had authority, who controlled a necessary information channel, what
each actor sought, what could be withheld, available substitutes, the agreement
sought, and what the public record can actually establish.

The existing [Ukraine imagery-access map](../archive/2026-09-21-information-cooperation/evidence/UKRAINE.md) offers a
concrete bargaining episode, but concurrent weapons and diplomatic pressure
prevent a clean estimate of the separate information effect. A PESCO or
High North arrangement could address autonomy/participation if negotiation and
release-rule evidence exists; a programme announcement alone is insufficient.

Begin with process tracing and rival explanations. A network representation is
useful only if the edges identify actual access, authority or dependence.
Centrality alone does not measure political influence. Consider a small
simulation only after evidence establishes a decision problem and defensible
assumptions; it would establish conditional model results, not real-world effects.
