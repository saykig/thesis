# AI military decisions

**Recorded:** 5 November 2026; expanded 10 November 2026. **Status:** research interest, not a selected paper question or case.

## Questions

- How does AI decision support change officers' judgment in theater-level decisions under time pressure? When is human approval meaningful?
- Which military instruments shorten particular steps in the OODA cycle or targeting process, and which steps still require human interpretation or authorization?
- How might faster sensing, recommendations and action affect crisis signaling, deterrence and escalation? Under what conditions would they have no such effect?
- What would reliance on private providers mean for European defence decisions, data access, evaluation, model changes and operator training?

OODA (observe, orient, decide, act) and the targeting “kill chain” describe different processes. A faster link between sensors and weapons does not by itself show that human judgment disappeared or that deterrence changed. The European focus will require a documented deployment and an identifiable decision or crisis episode.

## Research areas

1. **Decision process:** trace sensing, fusion, recommendations, authorization, action and feedback; identify where officers can question or override the system.
2. **Institutions and dependence:** map who controls data, interoperability, models and access across European states, NATO and private suppliers.
3. **Strategic effects:** test whether shorter decision windows alter signaling, misperception, escalation risk or deterrence by denial in a specified setting.
4. **Human and legal constraints:** assess trust, automation bias, accountability, civilian/defensive/offensive distinctions and applicable rules without assuming one legal regime covers all uses.
